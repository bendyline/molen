# Sounds v2: a plan

Written 2026-11-26 from a review of the audio engine, the `molen.sounds` pack and the Earth
soundscape, and worked through the same day: see [Status](#status) for what each phase delivered,
where it departed from the plan, and what is open. It extends [README.md](README.md) (the pack and
its pipeline) and the [audio guide](../../docs-src/guide/audio.md) (how hosts use it).

All phases were worked through on 2026-11-26. **Nobody has listened to any of it yet.** Every
choice of recording, trim, gain and curve was made from descriptions, measurements and tests; the

The first in-app report the same day ("birds chirping and city noises" while an F-15 took off)
found three faults the headless tests had missed, all fixed:
- The pack named its role `soundProfiles`, which the pack schema rejects (roles are kebab-case),

- The flight "rushing air" layer was `ambience.wind`, a park recording whose birds and traffic
  grew louder with airspeed. Flight without a vehicle now plays the generated `aircraft.wind`;
  aircraft keep the wind layer of their own profile.

Rendered wildlife is not voiced, except that crows are scheduled where the pack has local crows.

## Summary

The audio engine is sound. The world it plays is thin, and in several places wrong:

- **Most things that move are silent.** 2 of 54 aircraft have an engine sound. All 112 petrol and
  diesel road vehicles share two idle recordings, pitched up and down. Boats, motorcycles,
  electric cars, light rail and every vehicle the player is not driving make no sound at all.
- **Where and when a sound plays is decided by six boxes on the map and the weather preset.**
  There is no season and no climate. That is why Seattle gets Ontario cicadas and Tokyo gets
  Bengaluru's horns.
- **Nothing depends on the space the listener is in.** A cabin is one flat gain on the ambience
  beds. There is no muffling, no tunnel, no building interior, no Doppler, and no masking of
  quiet sounds by loud ones.

v2 is four pieces, in this order:

1. **Stop the wrong sounds** with rule and data changes only. No new recordings.
2. **A mix that knows where the ears are**: listener spaces (cabin, building, tunnel), an outside
   bus that is filtered as well as lowered, Doppler, masking, a volume control that follows
   loudness.
3. **Sound profiles**: several layers per vehicle, chosen by family (a low-bypass jet with
   reheat, a PT6 turboprop, a V8, an outboard), so every vehicle has a voice and the voice
   follows what the simulation is doing.
4. **A soundscape catalog**: every nature and street recording carries where, which months, what
   hours and what temperatures it belongs to, with its evidence, the way the birdsong catalog
   already does for eight recordings. Horns, crossing signals and sirens become events with a
   regional rate, not something baked into a looping bed.

Every recording stays CC0 or public domain with a verified source record. See
[Sourcing](#sourcing).

### How the engine is built

- An entity has one `audioSource`, so one loop. There is nowhere to put a second layer: no
  separate exhaust, tires, wind, skid or reheat.
- The graph is voice gain → panner → bus → master → limiter. The only filter is the helicopter
  rotor tone. There is no Doppler, no reverb and no loss of treble with distance.
- Neither host reports `indoors`, and tunnels (`sampleTerrainTunnel`) are not passed to audio, so
  walking into a building or driving through a bore changes nothing.
- Regions are six latitude and longitude boxes. Tel Aviv, Cyprus and Tehran fall in "africa";
  Hawaii and Miami fall in "latin-america".
- Insect and night activity come from daylight, rain and the weather's temperature. Traffic
  activity is `0.4 + 0.6 × daylight`: it follows the sun, not the clock.
- The birdsong catalog (8 recordings) replaces the regional bird beds everywhere, so most of the
  world has no birds.
- Beds are 30-second loops (14 seconds for Cairo) rotated every 83 to 119 seconds within a pool.
  A pool of one never rotates.
- `fetch.mjs` downloads Freesound's 128 kbps MP3 previews and the build encodes them to MP3
  again. That is tolerable for birdsong and poor for broadband sources such as jets and rain.

### What v1 does well

Keep all of this:

- The director is pure and deterministic, runs headlessly (`molen audio plan`), and never touches
  a state hash.
- Every clip has a source page, author, license, verification date and source hash, and the bank
  refuses anything that is not CC0.
- The birdsong catalog: bounds, habitats, monthly activity, time of day and evidence links per
  recording. v2 generalizes exactly this.
- Foley follows the simulation: doors and wipers from actuator travel, flap and gear motors,
  the touchdown chirp, twelve walking surfaces, rotor loading on the helicopter.
- Loudness is normalized at build time (-20 LUFS ambience, -18 LUFS engines and music).

## Design

### 1. The mix

**Listener space.** The host reports where the ears are, not just a mode: `open`, `cabin`,
`building`, `tunnel`, each with an enclosure amount from 0 to 1. A vehicle's cabin has two
figures by body style: isolation in dB and a cutoff frequency (a luxury saloon about -30 dB and
600 Hz; a pickup -22 dB; a convertible with the top down or a motorcycle close to 0; an airliner
flight deck; a fighter canopy; a doors-off OH-6).

**Two buses for the world.** Everything outside (beds, other vehicles, the own vehicle's exterior
layers) goes to an `outside` bus with one low-pass filter and one gain for the whole bus. The
own vehicle's interior layers, cabin foley and rain on the roof bypass it. One filter per bus
costs nothing measurable, where one per voice would.

**Masking.** A loud nearby source lowers the quiet beds. The director already estimates each
voice's audible level for its voice budget; the same figure ducks birds and insects under a
running engine. This is what makes a jet cockpit sound like one even in an outside view.

**The chase view.** Today it is fully outside. The recommendation: outside perspective, the own
vehicle's exterior layers held at a fixed near distance however far back the camera sits, and
beds ducked by masking. See [Decisions](#decisions-for-the-owner).

**Doppler.** The director already tracks each emitter's and the listener's motion. Pitch is
multiplied by `c / (c − radial speed)`, clamped. It is what makes a flyby or a passing car
sound like one.

**Distance.** Treble falls with distance (a low-pass on spatial voices beyond about 100 m, using
the per-voice filter the rotor tone already has). Sound delay over distance is left out.

**Reverb.** One shared convolver with a send per space: tunnel, street between tall buildings,
room. The impulse responses are generated by a script in the repository (shaped decaying noise),
so they are ours to dedicate to the public domain.

**Volume and user mix.** The master control follows loudness (a decibel taper), and the hosts
offer Master, Vehicles, World, Music and Interface, which map to buses. Qualla's Audio menu
needs the same controls on its side.

**Voice budget.** Earth holds 16 loops today. Layered vehicles and nearby emitters need about 40
on a desktop, fewer on a phone, with classes: own vehicle, then near emitters, then beds.

### 2. Sound profiles

A **profile** is a named set of layers. Each layer is a sound with its own gain and pitch
drives, a `when`, and a perspective (`interior`, `exterior` or both). Profiles live in the sound
pack, in a new document the pack provides, and are matched to an entity by the model it renders
(`renderable.ref`), with a fallback rule on the spec (propulsion, bypass ratio, reheat, engine
count, body style, fuel).

Keeping profiles in the sound pack and out of the entity type documents matters: the type
documents are asset-build inputs, and editing their generators costs hours of regeneration.
With profiles in the pack, sound work never rebuilds a model, and a project can re-voice the
fleet by replacing one document. `audioSource` stays as it is for simple scenes.

**Layers every powered vehicle shares**, driven by state the kernel already publishes:

| Layer | Signal |
| --- | --- |
| Engine, in bands that crossfade by rpm | `vehicleState.rpm`, `engineLoad`; `aircraftState.rpm` |
| Tires on the surface (asphalt, gravel, dirt, snow, wet) | speed, mapped surface |
| Wind on the body or airframe | speed or airspeed |
| Skid | `vehicleState.skid`, `lateralSpeed` |
| Gear change | `vehicleState.gear` changing |
| Suspension and landing thumps | `bodyHeaveRate`, `wheelLoad` |
| Horn | a player key; timbre by region and vehicle class |
| Rain on the roof | precipitation, cabin only |

**How to make an engine.** Three ways, and this needs a spike judged by ear:

1. *Recorded bands*: three or four loops of one engine at steady speeds, crossfaded, so no loop
   is shifted more than about a quarter. Best realism; CC0 sets of one engine at several speeds
   are rare.
2. *Synthesized*: a firing-order pulse train through exhaust resonances, in an AudioWorklet.
   Cylinder count and layout give each car its own voice from data we hold. Free of licensing;
   can sound like a toy if done carelessly.
3. *Hybrid*: a recorded texture for character with synthesized harmonics tracking rpm.

Jets and turbines are the easy case for synthesis: a jet is shaped broadband noise plus a
turbine whine whose pitch follows spool speed. Reheat is a second, lower noise layer on
`aircraftState.afterburner`. Recommended: hybrid, with recordings where a clean CC0 or federal
source exists.

### 3. The soundscape catalog

Replace the `earth:region:*` tags and the six boxes with a catalog document,
`molen/earth-soundscape@1`, modelled on `birdsong.json`. One entry per recording:

- `bounds`: boxes or polygons where it may play, kept near where it was recorded when it is a
  mixed chorus
- `habitats`, `months` (northern and southern written separately), `activity` by time of day
- `temperature`: the range it needs (cicadas call from about 22 °C; frogs chorus on mild wet
  nights)
- `evidence` and `coverage`, as birdsong has

The director needs one new input: a temperature for the place and month when the weather gives
none. A small latitude, month and elevation estimate is enough to keep cicadas out of January;
it also gives weather presets a sensible default.

What changes for the listener:

- **Seattle**: no cicadas. Pacific chorus frogs on spring nights, crickets on late summer nights,
  wind in conifers, gulls and water at the shore, crows.
- **Japan**: cicadas in July to September only, with the evening species at dusk; crossing
  signal melodies; almost no horns.
- **Birds everywhere**: where no curated profile fits, fall back to the regional bed instead of
  silence, and grow the catalog from 8 toward 60 to 80 profiles.
- **Wind** takes its character from what it blows through (conifers, broadleaf, grass, open
  ground, buildings), chosen from tree cover the scatter layer already knows.
- **Water** distinguishes surf, lake lapping, harbor and river.
- **Rain** by surface (leaves, pavement, water, roof), **thunder** with lightning, and **snow
  cover** hushing the high end of everything.

### 4. Streets and people

**Traffic is two things.** A road bed with no horns in it, chosen by road class and speed
(motorway hiss, city stop and go, a quiet residential street), and separate **events** the
director schedules: horns, sirens, a bus pulling away, a motorcycle passing. Each event has a
rate per minute that depends on a traffic culture, the density of ambient traffic and the local
clock. The schedule is a hash of seed, place and time bucket, so a replay hears the same horns.

| Culture | Horn rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| South Asia, Egypt, Vietnam, West Africa | High | Rickshaw and scooter horns, truck air horns |
| China, Southeast Asia, Latin America, southern Europe | Medium | Many small motorcycles |
| North America, northern Europe, Australia | Low | |
| Japan, Nordics | Very low | Crossing melodies and train crossing bells instead |

Traffic level follows the local hour (two peaks, a trough at 3 am), not daylight.

Around it: pedestrian crossing signals by country, train horns and crossing bells by region,
sirens by region (wail, hi-lo), tram bells, harbor sounds (gulls, halyards, a ferry horn),
airports (distant jets), and indistinct crowd murmur where pedestrians are dense. Recorded
speech and music are excluded; see [Sourcing](#sourcing).

### 5. Emitters in the world

The nearest few ambient cars, trains, boats and aircraft get real voices from their profiles,
with Doppler, so a car passes and a jet crosses overhead. Rendered wildlife calls from where it
is drawn. The voice budget's classes keep this bounded: perhaps six near emitters on a desktop
and two on a phone, the rest left to the bed.

### 6. Tools

- **`molen audio atlas`**: the probe above as an op. Place, date, hour and weather in; beds,
  events and levels out. A table of about 40 reference places by four seasons, day and night, is
  pinned by a test, with claims written as assertions: no cicadas in Seattle in any month, horn
  rate in Tokyo below a ceiling, in Bengaluru above a floor.
- **Coverage test**: every boardable type has a profile; the list of silent types is an allowlist
  that may only shrink.
- **Offline render**: a scenario rendered to a WAV in a headless browser (`OfflineAudioContext`)
  for listening and for loudness and spectrum measurement. Renders stay in `.artifacts/`.
- **Sound bench**: a local page to audition a profile with rpm, throttle, speed and perspective
  sliders, and a soundscape by place, month and hour. Judging these is the owner's ear; the
  bench makes that quick.

## Sourcing

The owner's rule: every sound is 100% sourced, and public domain or Creative Commons free for
commons use.

**Accepted in `molen.sounds`** (the bank's CC0 policy widens to exactly these):

- CC0 1.0
- Public domain as a work of the United States government, checked per item
- Synthesis and recordings made for this repository, dedicated CC0, with the generating script
  in Git

**Where to look**

| Source | Good for | Terms, and what to check |

**Not accepted**: the BBC archive; Pixabay (its
[license](https://pixabay.com/service/license/) bars distributing a sound on its own, which a
sound pack does); Zapsplat, Mixkit and similar "royalty-free" sites; Sonniss bundles;
[xeno-canto](https://xeno-canto.org/about/terms) recordings under NC or ND terms, which is most
of them; anything ripped from a game, film or video site.

**Rules for a clip**

- The existing record stays: page, author, license link, date verified, SHA-256 of the source.
- Add an archived copy of the license statement as seen (a web archive link), and a line on why
  the uploader is believed to be the author.
- No recognizable music and no intelligible speech in any recording. A street recording with a
  shop radio in it carries someone else's copyright whatever its uploader chose.
- A mixed chorus stays near where it was recorded. A single identified species may play across
  its documented range, with the range source in `evidence`.
- A foley stand-in is labelled as one in its description, as the tire chirp and servo motors
  are now.

## Costs and constraints

- **Size.** The pack is 50 MB: 30 MB of world beds, 10 MB of music. About 240 more recordings add
  roughly 60 to 90 MB. Split it into a core pack (vehicles, foley, interface), music, and world
  packs by region that a host fetches for where the listener is. The site has a 1 GB budget.
- **Encoding.** MP3 stays for Safari. Broadband sources get a higher bitrate and are encoded
  once, from originals.
- **Schema.** The profile document and the catalog are new formats; layers add fields the
  director reads. A schema change means `types gen` for four examples and three content
  projects.
- **Asset build.** `generate-aircraft.mjs` and `vehicle-dynamics.mjs` are generator inputs.
  Profiles resolved from the sound pack avoid editing them. If a spec field is wanted (blade
  count, cylinder layout), batch it with other model work.
- **Determinism.** The director stays pure. Event schedules come from hashes, never a clock or
  `Math.random`.
- **Phones.** Fewer voices, no convolver, and synthesized engines fall back to recorded loops
  where an AudioWorklet is unavailable.
- **Qualla** receives Earth view changes through a Molen release, and owns its Audio menu.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **"Depending on volume."** Which was meant: the volume control, the space you are in, or
   which vehicle it is? The plan covers all three.
2. **License line.** Recommended: CC0 and public domain only in `molen.sounds`, so apps built on
   Molen owe no credits. If a needed sound exists only under CC BY, it goes in a separate
   optional pack with credits shown. Never NC, ND or SA.
3. **Chase view.** Outside perspective with the own vehicle held near and beds masked
   (recommended), or keep the ears in the cabin.
4. **Birds without a profile.** Regional bed (recommended) or silence, which was the earlier
   choice.
5. **Engines.** Recorded bands, synthesized, or hybrid; to be chosen by ear after the Phase 3
   spike.
6. **Generated audio.** Scripted synthesis in the repository: yes. Generative AI services: the
   README allows them with a terms check. Recommended: exclude them from the default pack, since
   "100% sourced" is hard to stand behind for model output.
7. **A Freesound API key** for fetching originals.
8. **Human sounds.** Indistinct crowd murmur only (recommended). Station announcements, calls to
   prayer and church bells are characteristic and need a ruling on each.
9. **Loudness.** A reheat takeoff is painfully loud in life. Recommended: compress the range by
   default, with a "realistic" switch later if wanted.
10. **Horn key**, given the recent key remap.
