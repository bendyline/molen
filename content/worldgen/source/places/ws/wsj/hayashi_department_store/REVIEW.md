# Hayashi Department Store exterior review

The ordinary geographic model `hayashi_department_store` follows the five-vertex native building footprint and the matching named POI, both ID 35184576076528. The article pin is west of the building; nearby 11 m shop polygons are unrelated. The native northeast entrance chamfer is retained with photographed rounded shoulders, four circular-window levels, a tall observation bay and stepped parapet. Two street wings have four bays, horizontal window bands, projecting concrete/iron balconies and a ground-floor arcade. The fifth-floor terrace remains open; the elevator house and rooftop shrine are separate masses. Neighboring shops/bank, traffic, street furniture, interior displays and temporary banners are excluded.

Five complete local originals and all 41 candidate thumbnails were inspected. The comprehensive restored 2015 exterior and 2014 upper detail control the public facades. The 2013 elevator-house roof and 2015 fifth-floor terrace supplement them; the 2009 exterior is a historical comparison. A full original rooftop photograph by HSIAO, WEN-CHIEN resolves a weathered single-lintel stone torii, paired open stone lanterns, low shrine remains and steps. It does not show a roofed shrine house. The Commons summary/upload date and EXIF date differ; neither establishes a current survey. Photo provenance and exact hashes are in `reference-images.json` and `supplemental-reference-review.json`; no original photo pixels are redistributed or used as textures.

The native generic map height remains recorded as 15 m. The approximately 17.4 m street roof, 21.7 m corner parapet, upper terrace arrangement, headhouse and shrine positions are explicit photographic visualization estimates. Unseen rear windows and fine coping/artwork remain generalized. The model preserves five main store floors and a smaller sixth-floor observation/roof level; it does not interpret the traditional five-storey nickname as five identical full floor plates. The native outline is quantized and does not survey wall/kerb edges.

Four authored detail levels preserve the silhouette, native base, roof terraces and main circulation. Distant geometry uses restrained opaque porthole/rail proxies; the three closer levels contain real porthole recesses and circular observation-rail holes, with separate opaque window backings. Geometry validation inspects actual Float32 source/imported GLBs, normals/winding/UVs, strict budgets, native floor coverage, arcades, balconies, roof/parapet air, torii opening and own floors. Source/detail hashes and actual inspected frames are recorded in `qa.json` after review. Bulk captures stay ignored.

The dry entrance datum and independent ground were fixed before geometry. The retained published third-party tile is attributed by its publisher to the 2024 NLSC 20 m DTM; the original government raster and private conversion were unavailable. Roughly 17.59 m pixels are interpolated to the 1 m review grid. Independent decoding reproduces all 66,049 original sampled values; this does not imply 1 m accuracy. All 205 native-perimeter contacts remain between the existing foundation bottom (-0.6 m) and floor (0.15 m), with source ground approximately -0.4262 to -0.0042 m relative to the entrance datum. No flattening, cutout, fitted base or whole-site pad is used. Another host DEM requires its own contact review.

Placement is a bounded source-ground preview with `replaceFootprint:false`. Shared flat-terrain captures check transforms, map overlay and standard brick/concrete/metal/granite/tile bindings; the independent source field is reviewed separately. This exterior reconstruction does not promise surveyed current access, a navigable interior, exact roof positions or physical-device performance. Reproduce the selected source with the command in `README.md`, then import, derive the four detail levels and run `verify-geometry.mjs` plus `terrain-sources/verify-derived-grid.py`.
