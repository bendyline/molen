# Arugbá Gate

Arugbá Gate is a sculpted earthen portal in the Ọ̀ṣun–Òṣogbo Sacred Grove. The entrance photograph shows an asymmetric opening, deep recesses and weathering along the flared base. The 2019 roadside photograph shows the shelter's raised gable tips and the low ridge between them. [Virtual visit](https://artsandculture.google.com/streetview/arugb%C3%A1-gate/rAEf1RfoVGbBUg) · [CyArk photograph](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/arugb%C3%A1-gate-susanne-wenger-and-adebisi-akanji/ewEG54QkAFFOhA)

The master and four viewer levels retain both lifted gables, the irregular passage, the rounded clay return, the concrete apron, the roadside pedestrian arch and both iron leaves. Triangular soffit panels use alternating reed directions. The clay face follows the deep asymmetric recesses around the passage. The photographed frontage has a low wall with concave clay reliefs and embossed figures and vessels across the gates. The closer tiers model the seated drummer, fish-tailed figure and scale rows, the hooded figure above paired bowls, the kneeling bearer under the round finial, and three standing figures beside the larger incised vessel. The leaves have rounded crowns and separate wheel feet. Six shared materials supply surface detail, including plain concrete for the apron.

The master and close-up model the bowls and rolled ridges as one continuous clay surface across the roadside wall and pedestrian arch.
