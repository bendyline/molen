# Gateway to Ojúbọ Òṣogbo

The gateway’s three openings, double-headed central arch, relief wall and worn stairs come from photographs published by the Adunni Olorisha Trust and Adunni Osun Foundation. The public First Palace courtyard gateway and the separate Arugbá Gate have distinct identities. [Shrine trust exhibit](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/BgURvVEBTRI0TA)

The mesh uses shared lime plaster, weathered limestone and sandstone. The clay is warm ochre with darker moss around the base. In the close-up and master, the upper wall edges and arch undersides curve into both faces. Shallow sculpted surfaces on the central piers distinguish the wide face and animal, the upright figures, and the reliefs facing the stairs. Two freestanding stones flank the opening opposite the stair flight. They retain their silhouettes in the district tier and gain carved eyes, arms and stacked faces in the street and close-up tiers. The photographs guide their proportions, weathering and relief depths. [Gateway detail](arch-preview.webp)

The stair-side landing now carries the connected pebble loop visible in the public panorama. The street tier shows its outline. The close-up and master include the individual stones in the shared limestone material. [Trust panorama](https://artsandculture.google.com/streetview/gateway-to-oj%C3%BAb%E1%BB%8D-%C3%92%E1%B9%A3ogbo/5QEmYQylsItRCQ)

The published 2022 camera places the gateway 46.4 m from the earlier courtyard estimate. The stair side faces 6 degrees east of north. The revised wall profiles follow the photographed contacts and openings. The stairs use a terrain sample just beyond the bottom tread. At all four viewer levels, the fifteen sampled tread points clear native ground. The finer relief depth and side profiles remain photographic estimates. [Placement report](placement-report.json)
