# Hekatompedon temple

An Archaic reconstruction of the poros temple on the Athenian Acropolis circa 560 BCE. The 47.2 × 24 m plan uses the published peristyle dimensions and bay widths. The decorative scheme uses the museum's surviving stone capital and east and west pedimental sculptures. The reconstructed west gable includes the lions and bull, Herakles and Triton, and the three-bodied figure with intertwined tails. [Reconstruction study](https://www.academia.edu/42150499/Constructing_Monumentality_at_the_Athenian_Acropolis_in_the_Early_6th_Century_B_C) · [Acropolis Museum west pediment](https://www.theacropolismuseum.gr/en/hekatompedon-west-pediment)

The reconstruction follows the southwest-site interpretation, using the later Parthenon outline to locate and orient the hypothetical earlier terrace. The 6 × 12 peristyle, column elevations, sanctuary plan, roof ornament and polychromy include interpretation. The foundation and exact ancient axis are unexposed. The standard elevation archive samples the modern hill. The local floor is an approximate visualization control. [Map frame](map-frame.json)
